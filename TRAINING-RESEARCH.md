# Training and framework research · November 10, 2026

This review used public course catalogs, issuer pages, and framework publications. Access labels describe the linked resource. Enrollment, lab entitlements, prices, employer recognition, and individual eligibility can vary. No paid learner account or provider API was inspected. Mappings are IgniteCyber curriculum recommendations and have no provider endorsement.

## How to choose training

Use the assessment to identify a competency gap. Choose one appropriate resource, produce the work sample called for in the roadmap, and decide separately whether an exam would help. For defense and government roles, verify the actual vacancy, contract, work-role code, proficiency level, and qualification matrix before paying for an exam.

## Framework update

| Framework | Catalog status and use |
|---|---|
| [NICE](https://www.nist.gov/itl/applied-cybersecurity/nice/nice-framework-resource-center/nice-framework-current-versions) and [ENISA ECSF](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/skills-and-competences/skills-development/european-cybersecurity-skills-framework-ecsf) | Retained as workforce lenses. NICE Components v2.2.0 and ECSF’s 12 role profiles inform role descriptions. The app does not reproduce complete task, knowledge, or skill mappings. |
| [NIST CSF 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) and [AI RMF 1.0](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) | Retained. CSF describes cybersecurity outcomes and AI RMF organizes AI risk work. The published AI RMF remains the basis of the catalog while its revision is tracked. |
| [NIST AI 600-1](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) and [draft Cyber AI Profile](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8596/iprd) | Added the published GenAI Profile to AI governance, privacy, and oversight mappings. The Cyber AI Profile is a watchlist reference and does not drive the mappings. |
| [MITRE ATT&CK](https://attack.mitre.org/resources/versions/), [ATLAS](https://atlas.mitre.org/), and [D3FEND](https://d3fend.mitre.org/) | Updated ATT&CK to v20.1. ATLAS remains the AI threat-behavior reference and D3FEND the defensive countermeasure reference. Links point to the publishers and no full datasets are bundled. |
| [OWASP LLM Top 10](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/), [Agentic Applications Top 10](https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/), and [ASVS](https://asvs.owasp.org/) | Replaced the 2025 LLM list with the 2026 edition. Added agentic risks and ASVS 5.0.0 for application and identity control verification. |
| [NIST SP 800-53](https://csrc.nist.gov/News/2025/nist-releases-revision-to-sp-800-53-controls), [SP 800-171](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/171/r3/final), and [SP 800-82](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final) | Added security and privacy controls, protection of controlled unclassified information in nonfederal systems, and OT safety and security guidance. |
| [CIS Controls](https://www.cisecurity.org/controls), [Google SAIF](https://saif.google/), [ISO/IEC 42001](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html), and [SANS AI guidance](https://www.sans.org/artificial-intelligence) | Retained as distinct safeguards, architecture, AI management, and portfolio planning lenses. |
| [DoD 8140 qualification matrices](https://public.cyber.mil/wid/dod8140/qualifications-matrices/) | Remain the authoritative public-sector qualification reference for the applicable DoD work role and proficiency level. Provider suggestions do not change those rules. |

The catalog exports preserve the existing 26 roles and 169 role-to-competency mappings. New framework references and training options add context to those mappings without changing baseline scores, prerequisites, or study-hour estimates.
